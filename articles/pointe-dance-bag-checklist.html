What to Pack in Your Dance Bag for Pointe Class

Updated November 2026

Every pointe dancer has a story about the class where something went wrong: a ribbon that came loose mid-barre, a blister with no plaster in the bag, a pair of shoes left damp in a sealed pocket for a week. Almost all of these are packing problems, not bad luck. A well-organised dance bag turns small mishaps into a thirty-second fix.

This checklist covers what to carry for a regular pointe class, what to add for rehearsals and performances, and how to pack so your shoes dry properly and nothing gets lost at the bottom of the bag.

The short version: two things matter more than any single item. Keep your pointe shoes separate and ventilated, and keep a small repair-and-first-aid pouch that lives in the bag permanently, so you never have to remember to pack it.

The Essentials: Every Class

Pointe shoes, and a spare if you have one

Obviously. If you own a second pair, whether a newer pair for class or an older, softer pair kept as a backup, bring it to rehearsals and performances. Many dancers wear the newer pair for centre work and switch to a broken-in pair for barre, which also spreads the wear. For more on stretching out the life of a pair, see how to make pointe shoes last.

Toe pads and a spare pair

Toe pads wear out, go missing, and are always needed at the moment they cannot be found. Carry one spare pair beyond the pair you are wearing. If you cut your own pads from sheet material or use lamb's wool, bring a pre-cut set so you are not improvising in the changing room. Our accessories guide compares the main types.

Ballet shoes and any teacher-required items

Soft shoes for the first part of class, plus whatever your studio requires: character shoes, a skirt, a particular leotard colour. Check the dress code sheet and keep a copy in the bag.

Hair kit

Hair elastics, pins, a hairnet in your colour, and a small brush or comb. A bun that collapses mid-class is a distraction for you and a talking point for your teacher. Pins have a way of disappearing, so keep a dedicated stash in the bag rather than borrowing from your hair each morning.

Water bottle

Dehydration makes muscle cramps more likely, and cramps in the feet and calves are common during pointe work. A reusable bottle that seals properly is worth having, since a leak in a dance bag is a disaster for everything inside it.

The Repair Pouch

Pack these in a small zipped pouch that never leaves the bag. Restock it when you use something, not when you remember.

Sewing Kit

A needle, strong thread that matches your ribbons (dental floss or thick upholstery thread is popular because it resists fraying), and small scissors. Ribbons and elastics can come loose at the worst time. If you have never sewn them, our ribbon sewing guide walks through the process step by step.

Spare Ribbon and Elastic

A short length of spare ribbon and a piece of elastic let you replace a frayed tie or a snapped strap on the spot. Satin ribbon in a shade close to your tights is the usual choice, and a lighter and a darker option cover most skin tones.

Fray Check or Ribbon Sealant

Newly cut satin ribbon frays quickly. A dab of fray sealant on the ends keeps the cut edges tidy. Some dancers prefer to melt the end very briefly with a lighter, but that is best done at home and with care, not in a changing room.

Blister Plasters and Tape

Hydrocolloid blister plasters, plain surgical tape, and a few regular plasters take up almost no space and solve most foot problems before they get bad. If a blister keeps coming back in the same spot, that usually points to a fit or padding issue, which is worth raising at your next fitting. See the foot care guide for how to treat blisters and when to see a doctor.

Toe Spacers or Gel Sleeves

Optional, but many dancers like to keep gel toe sleeves or small spacers on hand for sore or crowded toes. They are a comfort item rather than a substitute for properly fitted shoes, so treat persistent crowding as a reason to revisit your fit.

Warm-Ups and Recovery

  • Warm layers. Leg warmers, a wrap cardigan or a sweater, and warm-up socks or booties. Cold muscles are more injury-prone, and feet that start cold take longer to warm up.
  • A spare leotard and tights. Particularly useful before rehearsals or performances, when a ladder or a spill would otherwise end your day.
  • Foot roller or massage ball. Rolling the sole of the foot after class eases tension in the arch. A small ball fits in a side pocket.
  • Resistance band. A light band is flat and weighs nothing, and it lets you do ankle and foot strengthening in a spare few minutes. See strengthening exercises for pointe for ideas.
  • A snack. Something simple with carbohydrate and a little protein, such as a banana, a cereal bar, or nuts. Long rehearsal days and low energy do not mix well with pointe work.

A Proper Dance Bag

Look for a bag with a separate, ventilated compartment for shoes, an outer pocket for wet or dirty items, and a padded strap you can carry comfortably. A bag that is too small is the most common reason things get crushed or forgotten, so size up if you carry both soft and pointe shoes plus a change of clothes.

Extras for Rehearsals and Performances

  • Stage makeup and remover. If you are performing, bring everything, including makeup wipes. Keep it in a separate pouch.
  • Safety pins and a few hair grips. Costume fixes are a performance-day staple.
  • Spare tights, in more than one shade. Costume requirements vary, and tights ladder without warning.
  • Printed schedule and contact numbers. Phones die, and backstage Wi-Fi is unreliable.
  • Tape for the shoes. Some dancers use a little tape to secure ribbon knots, but check with your teacher or company before changing how your shoes are finished.
  • A change of comfortable clothes. You will want them for the trip home.

How to Pack It

  1. Pointe shoes go in last and come out first. Take them out of the bag as soon as you arrive home, and let them air. Never leave damp shoes zipped inside a bag overnight.
  2. Use pouches for small items. One for the repair kit, one for hair, one for toiletries. Pouches stop everything migrating to the bottom, and make it quick to check you have it all.
  3. Keep wet and dry apart. Damp leotards and tights belong in the outer pocket or a separate bag, not against your shoes.
  4. Do a quick check each week. Replace the plasters you used, refill the pins, and take out the old snack wrappers. Five minutes on a Sunday evening saves a scramble on Monday.
  5. Pack the night before. Rushed morning packing is how shoes get left on the radiator.

A One-Page Checklist

If you want something to tick off, here is the whole list in one place:

  • Pointe shoes (and a spare pair, if you have one)
  • Soft ballet shoes
  • Toe pads, plus a spare set
  • Sewing kit, spare ribbon and elastic, fray sealant
  • Blister plasters, tape, and gel sleeves
  • Hair elastics, pins, hairnet, brush
  • Water bottle and a snack
  • Warm-ups: leg warmers, cardigan, socks
  • Spare leotard and tights
  • Foot roller and a resistance band
  • Mesh bag for drying shoes

The Bottom Line

A good dance bag is mostly about preparation: a small repair pouch that always lives in the bag, shoes that can breathe, and a short weekly reset. None of it is expensive, and all of it saves class time and stress. Start with the repair pouch and the mesh bag, then add the rest as you find what you actually reach for.
